# STRATEGY-WINNERS: inside our own strategies, what do the best trades have in common? — registered 20 Nov 2026, before the run
Owner: "look for stocks that did well in these strategies and then see the common factors." Descriptive, winners first, same split as the other studies. Paper only; not advice.
## Strategies (India, trades exactly as tested before; signals 2011-02..2023-11 so every trade has its full window)
HL-1/M3 (HUNT-1 M3), M1 (HUNT-1 M1), HL-2/M4 (HUNT-1 M4), each: green market, close at a 252-day high, 35% trailing stop, 780-session cap, cost 1.3%, next adjusted open. GC growth combo on raw prices (month-end: bottom-fifth raw price, top-fifth 6-month momentum, top-fifth traded-value trend; 35% trail, 780 cap, cost 2.0%).
## Winners
W10 = the best tenth of trades by net return inside each strategy and half; W3 = net return >= +200% (3x). Comparison group = all other trades of that strategy and half.
## Factors at entry (percentile rank against the liquid group of the last month-end on or before the signal; liquid = Rs 2 crore, raw price >= Rs 20)
6-month return, 12-1 return, volatility (126-session), drawdown from the 2-year high, distance below the 252-day high, traded value, raw price, traded-value trend, tightness (20-session over 100-session average move), signal-day volume ratio, 60-session range, distance above the 200-day mean, 6-month return minus Nifty 500's; plus market state (Nifty 500 drawdown from its 252-day high), entry year, and industry (current list only, 501 names, shown with coverage).
## Reading rule
No pass bar (nothing is selected). For every factor: AUC = chance a winner has the higher factor value than a non-winner (0.5 = no difference). Halves: signals 2011-02..2017-12 and 2018-01..2023-11. A factor is called "common" only if its AUC is on the same side of 0.5 by at least 0.05 in both halves and in at least two of the four strategies for W10. Anything common is a hypothesis for a registered test, not a rule.

## Result (20 Nov 2026; one run, no amendments)
Trades with features: M1 3,874, M3 2,964, M4 523, GC 1,774. Best tenth (W10) of trades earned at least +125% to +262% net depending on strategy and half (median W10 +182% to +395%) against a median trade of -3% to +14%.
Factors: within every strategy the best trades are barely separable by anything known at entry. AUCs sit between 0.35 and 0.64 and mostly flip between the halves (M1 12-1 return 0.62 then 0.46, price 0.56 then 0.42; M3 volatility 0.37 then 0.49, small size 0.40 then 0.51; M4 too few winners, 19 and 33). The only factors on the same side by at least 0.05 in both halves: for GC, closeness to the high and a shallower 2-year drawdown (AUC 0.57/0.60 and 0.56/0.60, the winners were less beaten down within the cheap, strong group); no factor is common to two or more strategies, so by the registered reading nothing is "common".
What does cluster: TIME and THEME. Winners come in waves: M1 2021 alone supplied 101 of 211 second-half winners (14% of that year's trades against 3% in 2018 and 2% in 2019), 2012-14 supplied 118 of 177 in the first half; M3 2020-23 supplied 170 of 174; GC 2022-23 supplied 67 of 102 and 2018-19 none. Where the industry is known (the list covers 16-45% of trades, current Nifty-500-style names only, so read as direction): first-half winners over-represented in Automobiles and auto components (1.6x to 4.7x), Consumer durables (1.5x to 2.2x), Chemicals (1.9x to 2.0x); second-half in Capital goods (1.4x to 2.0x), Chemicals (1.3x to 1.7x), Power (2.1x), Consumer durables (1.4x to 4.8x), Realty (4.2x), Consumer services (1.8x).
Reading: the best trades inside our own strategies are not stock-quality outliers; they are whichever theme leads the cycle (autos, consumer durables and chemicals in 2012-14, capital goods, power, chemicals, durables and realty in 2020-23) bought in the right phase of a strong market. That points to group (industry) strength and market phase as the thing to test, not another stock-level screen. Not tested here: the industry list is current-only and too sparse early on, so a registered test needs a historical industry map (or a proxy such as co-movement clusters) first.
