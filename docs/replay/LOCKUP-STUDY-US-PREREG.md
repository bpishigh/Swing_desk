# US IPO lock-up expiry event study: pre-registration (U-6, 17 Nov 2026)

Written before any price was read. One run, report only: nothing is switched on from it (C-2). Paper only; not a financial adviser. Owner's question (17 Nov 2026): do prices rise or fall around lock-up expiry.

**Method (mirrors the India anchor study, C-214).** Listing = the first session a symbol appears in the stored US daily files (data/us/hist, from 17 Oct 2016) after 1 Dec 2016; symbols present earlier are pre-existing. Expiry proxy = listing date + 180 calendar days (the common lock-up length; the real date is in each prospectus and was not parsed). Window = 7 sessions before to 4 sessions after the session of the expiry proxy (11 sessions); window return = close at the last session before the window to close at its last session. Names with fewer than 260 stored sessions after listing are skipped. Baseline: the same name's average 11-session return over non-overlapping windows from 20 sessions after listing, skipping windows that touch the expiry window. Also reported against SPY over the same window. Reported: n, mean and median window return, share negative, mean against own average, mean against SPY, and the mean return of the 7 sessions before and the 4 after separately. Groups fixed now: all listings; listed 2017 to 2020; listed 2021 onward.

**Pass rule.** None: a count and a description. A mean under about 1 percentage point is within noise at these sample sizes.

**Known limits, printed in the report.** (1) Survivor bias: all 2,279 stored US symbols are still trading, so IPOs that later failed or were acquired are absent, which flatters every group. (2) First appearances are not all IPOs: spin-offs, SPAC mergers, ticker changes and direct listings also appear. (3) 180 days is a proxy: early-release terms, staged expiries and price-triggered releases move the real date. (4) Many US lock-ups end after earnings, so the window can contain a results reaction. (5) The 2021 to 2022 IPO boom and crash sit in the second group.
