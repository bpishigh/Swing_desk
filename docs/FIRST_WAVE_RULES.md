## Shared terms (apply to every book unless the block says otherwise)

- **Universe.** Exactly the list the C-129 sleeve uses (`eligible` in `src/backtest/ind-base.ts`, then the top 200): as-of the signal date, close at least Rs 20, at least 260 sessions of history, 20-session median traded value at least Rs 5 crore, the 200 highest by that 20-session median value; delisted names included in history. Every book here uses this one definition, so a variant differs from its parent only in what the block says. (The six-month median in the 11 Oct screener brief belongs to the retired scan, not the sleeve.)
- **Signal and fill.** Signal on a session's close; simulated fill at the next session's open (C-139); the existing tiered costs (`tierCost`).
- **Start.** First signal on the first session close after the owner freezes the row. Until then the desk shows "not started".
- **Sizing.** Equal weight, at most 20 names, at most 5% each; the rest is cash, credited at the liquid-fund rate (C-175). (Long-hold: 5% per name keeps a 25% stop-out within 1.25% of the book, C-159.)
- **Stops (C-159).** Long-hold books: 25% trailing on closing prices, sold at the next open, per-name cap 5%, re-entry on the next valid signal, gaps past the stop logged. Short-hold books (60 sessions or less): the tighter of 3 x ATR(20) and 8% below entry, trailing on closes, filled at the next open.
- **Benchmarks on every book (owner, 10 Nov 2026).** Four lines: Nifty 200, Nifty 500, the monthly momentum twin of the sleeve (C-151), and the Nifty200 Momentum 30 index fund NAV net of its dividend yield (the instrument the desk would hold instead; the C-173 cost-difference condition is read against it). The AMFI NAV file already feeds the fund line for the sleeve.
- **Monthly read.** On the first session of each month the book writes a scorecard: net return since start, excess against each benchmark over the month, since start and trailing 6 months, drawdown, closed trades, average net return per closed trade, turnover and cost paid, stop-outs, and the kill-line status. A monthly read gives Continue, Watch or Kill only (C-173). Adopted is decided only at a thick read (12 months and 60 closed trades) under the C-173 pass standard; until then figures are marked "thin". Only the owner marks Adopted, Rejected or Parked.
- **Kill line (C-159, amended by C-174).** Relative drawdown: the book's fall from its own peak measured against the benchmark's move over the same window, 20%; trailing 6-month net return more than 5 points below the benchmark at two consecutive monthly reads; zero or negative average net return after 40 closed trades. One line is Watch; the drawdown line alone or any two is Kill.
- **Pass standard (C-173).** Monthly reads give Continue, Watch or Kill only. Adopted needs a thick read (12 months, 60 closed trades) and all of: the block-bootstrap 90% interval on the mean monthly net excess over the benchmark or parent twin has a lower bound above zero; the point estimate exceeds the cost difference; the read states its power; and the read reports how many rules and variants have been tried in total.
- **Stopped parent (C-173).** The comparison parent of C-162, C-167 and C-168 is a new book: the monthly-review sleeve with the C-159 long-hold stop (25% trailing, 5% per name). The variants inherit that stop. The unstopped C-151 twin stays as a reference line.
- **Known limit: slippage.** The cost model is the sleeve's `tierCost` (round-trip cost by traded-value tier) with no separate slippage tier. The owner has not decided whether to add one. Until then C-163, C-166 and C-172, which trade more often, carry the limit and their costs may be understated.
- **What to expect from a thick read.** With 12 to 24 months of monthly data, the C-173 interval condition can only confirm a large effect. As an illustration (not measured here): if monthly excess return against an index has a standard deviation of about 3%, a lower bound above zero at 24 months needs a mean excess of roughly 12 points a year; against a parent that shares most holdings, with a standard deviation of about 1%, roughly 4 points a year. Most thick reads are therefore expected to say Continue, and that is the standard working as intended, not a failure of the books.
- **Self-improvement.** One champion; at most two pre-written shadow variants; a swap only on fresh data under a rule written in advance, at most once a year; the number of variants tried is recorded. The variants named in each block are the only ones.
- **Evidence status.** Each block says what is borrowed from outside research and what is unknown. None of the blocks has been run on this repo's data.
- **Not tested.** No block has been tested on India data. The owner has chosen forward paper testing as the evidence. The two studies (S1, S2) are the only history tests in this wave.

## Books

### C-160: Industry momentum (India)
Question: does holding the strongest industries beat holding the strongest single stocks (the sleeve)? Signal: for each industry in `data/india/segments.json` with at least 5 eligible names, the median 12-1 return of its eligible names (close 21 sessions ago over close 252 sessions ago, less 1), as-of. Hold the 5 strongest names (by own 12-1 return) of each of the top 4 industries: 20 names at 5%. Review: first session of each month. A held name stays while its industry is in the top 6 and it ranks in the top 8 of that industry. Hold: long. Stop: 25% trailing. Borrowed evidence: industry momentum is documented in US data (Moskowitz and Grinblatt, 1999); India is unknown. Variants (pre-written): median of the 6-1 return; top 3 industries by 6 names. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-161: 52-week-high proximity (India)
Question: do stocks closest to their 52-week high keep outperforming? Signal: close divided by the highest close of the last 252 sessions; hold the 20 highest, 5% each. Review monthly; a held name stays while in the top 40. Long. Stop 25% trailing. Borrowed evidence: George and Hwang (2004), US. Variants: 126-session high; exclude names flagged by C-168 (MAX). Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-162: Low-turnover momentum (India), variant of the sleeve
Question: does removing the most heavily traded names from the sleeve's pool improve it? Signal: the C-129 volatility-adjusted score on the eligible list after dropping the top fifth of names by 60-session average traded value (the price-only stand-in; true turnover needs shares outstanding, which the loader does not yet carry). Otherwise the stopped parent's rule (C-173): monthly review, 25% trailing stop, 5% per name. Long. Stop 25% trailing. Borrowed evidence: low-turnover momentum in US studies; the stand-in is not the same measure. Variants: drop the top third; drop the top tenth. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-163: Donchian 55-session breakout (India)
Question: does a plain channel breakout work after Indian costs? Entry: close above the highest close of the previous 55 sessions. Exit: close below the lowest close of the previous 20 sessions, or the stop. If more than 20 names signal, take those with the highest 12-1 return. Up to 20 names at 5%. Short-hold stop (3 x ATR(20) or 8%, tighter). A name held past 60 sessions keeps that tight stop. Borrowed evidence: Turtle-type rules, mostly futures; stock evidence is mixed.  Expected stop behaviour, stated before the first signal: on this universe the 3 x ATR limb sets the stop on about 85% of signals (median 3 x ATR is 5.1% of price) and the 8% limb only keeps out the other 15%, whose 3 x ATR is above 8%, onto the stop-too-wide list. An 8% trailing stop was hit within 20 sessions on about half of entries in the stop-feasibility data, and a tighter ATR stop is hit at least as often, so frequent stop-outs are expected. Signals whose 3 x ATR is above 8% are skipped, not stopped at 8% (C-159), and the number skipped is shown on the scorecard, because they are the most volatile names. With a stop near 5% the stop will usually fire before the 20-session-low exit, so in practice the book is a 55-session breakout with a trailing stop of about 5%; that is an expectation to check, not a result. Variants (pre-written): the same entry with a flat 8% stop in place of the ATR stop (a wider stop on most names, which measures the stop question); the 20/10 channel. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-164: Weekly 12-1 positional momentum (India)
Question: does re-ranking weekly (Friday close) help or only add cost? Signal: 12-1 return as in C-160; hold the top 20, 5% each; a held name stays while in the top 40. Review each Friday (the last session of the week). Long. Stop 25% trailing. Borrowed evidence: momentum is documented at monthly horizons; weekly is unknown. Variants: fortnightly review; top-10 names at 5%. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-165: Trend template as a universe filter with a no-template twin (India), folded
Owner decision 10 Nov 2026: C-165 is not a separate book. The template is registered as a universe filter on the stopped monthly-review sleeve (C-173), and its twin is the same stopped parent without the filter, so the filter is the one thing changed. Filter: only names passing the C-135 template are eligible (close above the 150 and 200-day averages; 150 above 200; 200-day average above its level 21 sessions earlier; 50-day above 150 and 200; close above the 50-day; at least 30% above the 52-week low and within 25% of the 52-week high). Otherwise the sleeve rule, monthly review, long-hold stop. Evidence: C-135 passed in India (+1.19% a month, 8 of 9 years) and did not confirm in the US (+0.24%, interval touches zero); this is the forward confirmation for India only, and the effect overlaps the sleeve's own selection. Variants: the template without the 52-week-low condition. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read against its no-template twin.

### C-166: Turn-of-month (India)
Question: does the calendar effect around the month turn survive Indian costs? Entry: close of the fifth-to-last session of the month, buying the 20 most traded eligible names at 5%. Exit: close of the third session of the next month. Short-hold stop. The book is in cash the rest of the month, so cash share is reported. Borrowed evidence: turn-of-month effects in index returns, several markets; single-stock version unknown. Variants: fourth-to-last entry; exit on the second session. Expected cost, stated before the first signal: 12 round trips a year on names in the top cost tier (0.35% a round trip) is about 4.2% a year before slippage, so the book must earn more than that to pass. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-167: Volatility-targeted sleeve (India)
Question: does scaling the monthly sleeve's exposure to a volatility target improve return per unit of risk? Signal: the stopped parent's holdings (C-173), with exposure each review set to 15% divided by the book's own annualised volatility over the last 60 sessions (daily net return), capped at 100%; the rest is cash, credited at the liquid-fund rate (C-175). Long. The stopped parent's stop (25% trailing, 5% per name); the unstopped sleeve is not the comparison. Borrowed evidence: volatility-managed portfolios (Moreira and Muir, 2017), US; India unknown. Variants: 12% target; 20% target. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-168: MAX exclusion book (India)
Question: does excluding lottery-type stocks improve the monthly sleeve? Flag: a name is a MAX name if its largest one-day return over the last 21 sessions is in the top tenth of the eligible list. Signal: the stopped parent's rule (C-173) with MAX names removed before ranking. Long. Stop 25% trailing. The flag is also written to the nightly file for every name as information. Borrowed evidence: Bali, Cakici and Whitelaw (2011), US. Variants: top fifth; 63-session window. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-169: Multi-asset trend layer and risk-parity yardstick (India), equity and gold
Question: does a simple trend filter across Nifty 50 and gold cut drawdown, and at what return cost? Owner decision 10 Nov 2026: equity and gold now; a bond leg is added when a source is found. Assets: Nifty 50 (NIFTYBEES in the daily price files, price only, no dividends) and gold (GOLDBEES), both from 4 Jan 2010. Trend book: at each month end, hold each asset equally only while it closes above its 10-month average, else cash. Yardstick: the two assets weighted by inverse 60-session volatility, no filter. Cash is credited at the liquid-fund rate (C-175). Long. The 10-month average is the mean of the last ten month-end closes. After a stop-out the asset is bought again at the next month end if it is still above that average (C-159: re-entry on the next valid signal). The 10-month filter is the exit being tested; the 25% trailing stop (C-159, no exception) is a second exit on each asset and is expected to fire rarely. Judged on drawdown with the return cost stated, against the equal-weight hold of the same two assets. Borrowed evidence: Faber (2007), US and global. Variants: 12-month average; add the Nifty Midcap 100 index. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-172: Pullback in an uptrend (India)
Question: does buying the first up close after a short dip in a leading stock beat buying the breakout (C-163)? Universe: eligible names in the top momentum quintile by the C-129 score that also pass the C-135 template. Signal: a fall over 3 to 5 sessions, or a close touching the 20-day average, then the first up close; entry is that close's next open, never the dip itself. Exit: the stop, or the name leaving the top momentum quintile at a monthly review. Up to 20 names at 5%. Stop: short-hold rule (tighter of 3 x ATR(20) and 8%); signals whose 3 x ATR is above 8% are skipped and counted on the scorecard (C-159). No tightness or volume gate; volume ratio and tightness are logged on every signal so the book can show any split (build brief, stream 5). Source: the build brief, tier 2, stream 5; omitted from the first draft by mistake and added 9 Nov 2026. Borrowed evidence: trader practice, little formal evidence. Variants: a flat 8% stop in place of the ATR stop (wider than the ATR stop on most names, so it measures the stop question); the template condition dropped. Rejects it: the kill line, or failing the C-173 pass standard at a thick read.

### C-170: Base-rate study (S1, price only)
Question: how often do Indian stocks, delisted ones included, reach 3x or 5x within 5 years from any start date? Method: every stock-start date from 2011 on with 5 years of price afterwards; fully adjusted prices; delisted names count at their last price; counted by start year and by price and liquidity band. Run once, no tuning. Judged: reported only; it is the yardstick for every multibagger claim. Not tested: anything involving fundamentals.

### C-171: Price-only multibagger lift test (S2)
Question: does nearness to the 52-week high, with MAX names excluded, or a liquidity or size band, raise the share of stocks reaching 5x in 5 years above C-170's base rate? Method: as C-170, grouped by the three features, set before the run; lift reported with bootstrap intervals; years are blocks. Run once, no tuning. Judged: lift is reported as a ratio to the base rate with its interval; a result is called an effect only if the lower bound exceeds 1 in at least 60% of start years.
