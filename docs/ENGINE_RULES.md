# Swing Desk – Engine Rules (Source of Truth)

Oct 3, 2026 · @Owner

## How to use this document

This is the single source of truth for what the Swing Desk decides and why. If the code and this document disagree, this document is right and the code has a bug.

**Rules version:** R-2026.10.9 (R-2026.10.8 plus the removal of the India skipped-days wording, adopted 20 Oct 2026, C-73; R-2026.10.8 was R-2026.10.7 plus the sector zero-return test adopted 17 Oct 2026, C-58; R-2026.10.7 was R-2026.10.6 plus the automatic handling of untyped demergers adopted 15 Oct 2026, C-50; R-2026.10.6 was R-2026.10.5 plus the Legacy exit-test definition adopted 15 Oct 2026, C-41; R-2026.10.5 was the Swing Desk app v9 as of 3 Oct 2026, after two reviews, plus three corrections found in the third review, C-35 to C-37, and the Red setup list, OQ-8. The live Claude desk was patched to v9.1 on 3 Oct 2026 and now follows these rules, except that it counts weekdays and not exchange holidays until the GitHub build adds the calendar, C-39). Every rule change bumps this number and adds a row to the Change log.

**The change rule:**

1. Write the proposed change in the Change log first, with status Proposed, the reason and the evidence that motivates it.
2. Test it (backtest, replay or paper trade) and record the result in the same row.
3. Mark it Adopted, Rejected or Parked. Only Adopted changes edit the rule text above.
4. Change the code, then record the code version in the row, and re-export this document to docs/ENGINE\_RULES.md in the repo in the same commit. The Code and Architecture document is updated in the same step if data, settings or structure changed.

Rejected ideas stay in the log permanently so nobody re-tests them without new evidence.

**Scope:** the rules apply to both markets. Where India and the US differ, the difference is shown in a per-market table and the reason is given. Parameters live in settings files, never inside the code (see the Code and Architecture document).&#32;

**Precedence between documents:** this document wins over every other. The app's Rules tab (the playbook) is the trader-facing manual derived from the current rules version, and the Master Rules document and the scope of work are inputs and plans. If any of them disagree with this document, this document is right and the other one is updated, never the reverse.

## Principles

The desk is built on risk first, momentum second, and as little discretion as possible.

- **Market first.** The regime traffic light decides how much risk is allowed at all. A strong stock in a Red market is still a small position or no position.
- **Leaders in leading sectors.** Money is made in the strongest sectors and the strongest stocks inside them. Weak sectors are not traded.
- **Fixed risk per trade.** Every position is sized from the stop distance so a stop-out costs a fixed fraction of capital.
- **Rules decide, the trader executes.** The tool tells the trader what is allowed. It does not place orders.
- **Data accuracy beats rule cleverness.** A rule fed wrong numbers is worse than a simple rule fed right ones. Every number carries its source and date, and stale data blocks a decision rather than being silently used.
- **Same logic for both markets.** India and the US share one rule set. Differences are parameters, each with a stated reason.
- **Nothing is changed without a log entry.** See the change rule above.

## Regime traffic light

The regime is Green, Yellow or Red, computed on the market benchmark (India: Nifty; US: SPY) and taking the worst of the tests below.

| Test | Rule | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trend | Benchmark above both 50 and 200 SMA and the 50 SMA rising | Green. Above one average and below the other, or 50 SMA not rising: Yellow. Below both: Red |
| Distribution days | 5 or more in the last 25 sessions | Caps the regime at Yellow |
| Breadth | Share of stocks above their 50 SMA below 40% | Caps the regime at Yellow |
| Follow-through day | A confirmed follow-through day after a correction | Lifts Red to Yellow |
| Volatility (India) | India VIX above 20 | Halves the risk per trade |

**Exact definitions (from app v9):**

- **Benchmarks:** India uses Nifty 50 and Nifty 500; US uses SPY and QQQ. Each is read as Green (above 50 and 200 SMA, 50 rising), Mixed (above one average and below the other, or 50 SMA not rising) or Red (below both). The trend level is the lower of the two.
- **Distribution day:** the index closes down 0.2% or more on higher volume than the previous non-skipped session (a skipped day's volume is never the baseline, C-36). A day drops out after 25 sessions, or earlier if the index closes 5% above that day's close. Five or more active days cap the regime at Yellow. The US uses SPY volume with no expiry adjustment.
- **Skipped days:** none, in either market. The engine can skip a flagged day (the skip field on a tracker row), but no day is flagged: every session counts for distribution and follow-through, as in the US. C-72 (20 Oct 2026) tested excluding India's monthly expiry, Muhurat and after-holiday sessions and found no improvement, so the earlier India skip list was dropped (C-73).
- **Follow-through day:** after the index makes a new low, day 1 is the first close above the previous day's high. On day 4 or later, a close up 1.25% or more on higher volume than the previous session is the follow-through day. A new low before that restarts the count. Follow-through is only looked for inside a correction. The correction tracker arms when the benchmark closes 8% or more below its highest close of the last 60 sessions, or when the trend test reads Red (below both its 50 and 200 SMA), whichever comes first; it disarms when the benchmark closes above that 60-session high again. At arming, the low is the lowest low since the 60-session closing high, and day 1 and the day count are worked out from that low over the stored rows, not from the arming day; so a follow-through on the arming day itself is valid, and the rally-low failure test uses that seeded low and is never set above the true low. If a new low forms after arming, it replaces the seeded one and restarts the count. All follow-through state is cleared when the tracker disarms. A follow-through lifts Red to Yellow and nothing else: if the tracker is armed but the trend is Yellow, it is recorded and changes nothing. The 8% and 60 sessions are untested starting values (C-35).
- **Follow-through status:** Active when it happens, Confirmed when breadth goes above 50%, Failed if the index closes below the rally low or breadth has not passed 50% within 10 sessions. Sessions, here and everywhere in these rules, are counted on the market calendar: weekends and exchange holidays are not sessions (C-37). Active or Confirmed lifts Red to Yellow.
- **Breadth:** the share of a stock universe whose close is above its own 50-day average, in percent to one decimal. India: the Nifty 500 (NSE's current list) on stored NSE closes. US: the S&P 500 (the S5FI definition) on Nasdaq's public history. A stock needs 50 sessions of history and is left out (counted as skipped) when any one-day move inside its 50-day window is beyond 22%, which marks an unadjusted split or bonus. Today's list is used for past days, so past breadth carries survivorship bias. Below 40% caps the regime at Yellow.
- **India VIX:** above 20 halves risk per trade.

## Sector ranking

Only sectors that pass the gate are ranked, and only the top four are tradable that week (top two in Yellow).

1. **Gate.** The sector's 3-month return beats the benchmark's 3-month return, the sector's own 3-month return is above zero (C-58), and the sector is above its 50 SMA. In Red mode the 50 SMA test is dropped; the two 3-month tests still apply. A sector that is falling, but falling less than a falling benchmark, does not pass.
2. **Score.** 2 × 3-month return + 1-month return. Highest score ranks first.
3. **Top four** are kept each weekend.
4. **Confirmed or early.** Above the 200 SMA is confirmed (full size). Below it is early (pilot size only).
5. **Persistence.** A sector must appear in the top four on two consecutive weekends before it earns full trust. A new leader (a sector that jumps from outside the top 8 into the top 4 within three weeks) that has not yet persisted may be traded as a pilot only.
6. **Concentration.** Maximum 2 open positions per industry and 3 per sector.

**Data basis.** India sector returns and SMAs come from the official NSE index close file. US sector returns come from stored unadjusted closes. The US 200 SMA is computed from those stored closes, which can sit up to about 1.3% below published dividend-adjusted averages for dividend-paying ETFs. This is expected and is not a data error (see Change log C-14).

## Stock selection

Stocks are only considered inside sectors that rank in the current top group.

**US candidate screen (live in app v9, run weekly):**

| Test | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Pool | Top 10 holdings of each sector ETF in the top group (Yellow: 2 sectors, Green: 4) |
| Trend | Close above 50 and 200 SMA, and 50 SMA above 200 SMA |
| Leads its sector | 1-month return at least equal to its sector ETF's 1-month return |
| Liquidity | Average daily dollar volume of $50 million or more |
| Results | Next results date more than 10 trading days away. Unknown date is flagged, never passed |
| Setup and room | A permitted setup is in place, stop width is within the setup's limit, and there are 3R of room to the 50-day high |
| Freshness | Stock data dated before the screen date is excluded |

A stock is **Ready** when every test passes and **Watch** when trend, liquidity and results pass but no setup, room, stop width or relative strength is in place. The trader still checks the 3-month relative-strength line against SPY on the chart before buying.&#32;

**India stock scan (Chartink, manual until the GitHub build):** the Minervini trend template: close above 150 and 200 SMA, 150 above 200, 200 SMA rising versus a month ago, 50 SMA above 150 and 200, close above 50, at least 30% above the 52-week low and within 25% of the 52-week high, traded value over ₹10 crore a day, price over ₹50, market cap over ₹1,000 crore.

**Known limits:** the US screen uses 50-session highs and lows, not a full year. It reads holdings of the ETFs only, so smaller stocks outside the sector indexes are not found until the whole-market scan is built (Parked item P-1).

**Target rule (not yet built, see Change log C-20):** one trend template for both markets, applied to the whole liquid universe each night, with a relative-strength percentile rank. India and US use the same tests with different liquidity floors.

## Setups and entry calculation

| Id | Setup | Trigger above basis | Limit above trigger | Max stop width | Basis price | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Base breakout | 0.5% | 1% | 8% | Pivot (top of the base) | Stop: low of the last tight area |
| 1ipo | IPO base breakout | 0.5% | 1% | 10% | Pivot (top of the IPO base) | Listed 30 or more sessions ago (no IPO in its first 30 trading days) |
| 2 | Pullback to 20 EMA | 0.2% | 1% | 7% | High of the day that closed at or above the 20 EMA | Stop: pullback low. Skip if the pullback closes below the 50 SMA |
| 3 | Earnings gap | 0.2% | 1.5% | 8% | Gap-day high | Stop: gap-day low. Exempt from the results-date test. Not used in the US |
| 4 | Pullback to 50 SMA | 0.2% | 1% | 6% | High of the holding day | Stop: lowest low of the pullback. Skip if the pullback closes below the 50 SMA |

**Entry calculation (function ntCalc in app v9):**

1. Trigger price = basis price × (1 + trigger %). The basis is the pivot for a breakout or the pullback high for a pullback.
2. Limit price = trigger × (1 + limit %). Orders above the limit are not chased.
3. Stop = the setup's structural stop (US screen: the lowest low of the last 10 sessions). If that stop is closer than 1.5 × ATR(14) below entry, it is widened to entry − 1.5 ATR so ordinary noise does not stop the trade out.
4. Stop width must not exceed the setup's maximum. If it does, the trade is skipped, not resized.
5. Room: distance from entry to the nearest overhead resistance must be at least 3R in both the US and India (India ran at 3R from the start; C-112). A breakout with no overhead resistance passes (in the manual checklist, a resistance level of 0 or at or below the entry means none above).
6. Results: the next results date must be more than 10 trading days away.
7. Size comes from the sizing section below.

Why the markets differ: the room test is now 3R in both markets (C-112). The owner first stated 2R for India on 3 Oct 2026 because of the tax cost of US stocks, but the India scan and paper book always ran at 3R and a 2R trial was not adopted. Setup 3 (earnings gap) is skipped in the US because US results gaps of 20% happen and a stop cannot act overnight (C-25). The 3R test also covers the extra brokerage and slab-rate tax.

## Position sizing and limits per market

Shares = (capital × risk per trade) ÷ (entry − stop), then limited to 20% of capital per position. India VIX above 20 halves the risk per trade.

**India**

| Regime | Setups allowed | Sectors traded | Risk per trade | Max positions | Max open risk (new trades) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Green | 1, 1ipo, 2, 3, 4 | top 4 | 0.5% | 10 | 5% |
| Yellow | 1, 1ipo, 3, 4 | top 2 | 0.5% | 5 | 2.5% |
| Red | 1, 4 | top 4 | 0.25% | 2 | 0.5% |

**US**

| Regime | Setups allowed | Sectors traded | Risk per trade | Max positions | Max open risk (new trades) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Green | 1, 1ipo, 2, 4 | top 4 | 0.5% | 4 | 2% |
| Yellow | 1, 1ipo, 4 | top 2 | 0.5% | 4 | 2% |
| Red | 1, 4 | top 4 | 0.25% | 2 | 0.5% |

Values are copied from LIMITS and LIMITS\_US in app v9. The last column is the maximum total open risk for new trades, because the code compares open risk against capital times this percentage. It is not a position cap; the position cap is 20% of capital (next section). In Red the playbook allows pilots only, and the checklist enforces that, so the setup list for Red is setups 1 and 4 only, in both markets (owner decision 3 Oct 2026, OQ-8 closed; app v9 let Red accept every setup) (C-24, C-38).

A stock scoring Ready on the US screen is still sized by the normal New trade checklist; the screen never sizes a trade.

## Entry mechanics and book rules

These rules were already in app v9 but missing from this document until the 3 Oct 2026 review.

- **Two-part entry:** buy 60% of full size at the trigger. Add the other 40% after a close at +1R and move the stop on all shares to entry. If the first +1R close is already at +2R or higher (a gap), skip the add and sell the third under exit rule 5. Adds do not count toward the daily entry cap.
- **Pilot size:** risk is capped at 0.25% when the sector is a new leader that has not yet persisted, when the stock is a bottom-up leader (3 or more stocks of one industry passing the scans) in a sector that has not persisted, when the sector is below its 200 SMA (early), and in every Red market. A bottom-up leader skips the sector rank, persistence and 200 SMA checks (the industry evidence stands in for them) and trades at pilot size until its sector persists.
- **Red path:** a Red-market trade needs an RS leader (India: top 20% of Nifty 500 on 3- or 6-month return; US: RS line against SPY rising or at a new high) trading above its own 50 SMA, and one of the setups.
- **Entry order type: if ATR is above 3% of price, skip the intraday standing buy order. If the stock closes above the pivot in the top half of its day's range, place a limit buy at that close + 1% the next morning (India: an after-market order, AMO; US: a limit buy) and skip the trade if it does not fill. Never use a market order for a buy. In the trade record, method is amo for this close-confirmed entry and gtt otherwise. The stop is always a standing order placed the same evening as the fill**.
- **Daily cap:** at most two new entries a day, highest RS first.
- **Position cap:** no position above 20% of capital, even if the risk formula says more.
- **Sector rank:** the stock's sector must be in the top 4 (top 2 in Yellow).
- **Legacy book (India long-term holdings):** the exit test is a close below the 200 SMA or below the last major swing low. The last major swing low is the most recent confirmed pivot low on daily closes: a close that is the lowest of the 10 sessions on each side of it, confirmed only after 10 later sessions. The 200 SMA is the mean of the last 200 closes. Closes are first adjusted for splits, bonuses, large dividends, rights and demergers (a typed record that passes the price check first; otherwise a fall of 15% or more across the ex-date is used as the factor, and a smaller one drops the closes before the ex-date, C-44, C-50). The test gives no verdict where an action in the last 300 sessions cannot be adjusted, the latest close is over 7 days old, or fewer than 200 sessions exist and the swing low is not broken; a stock with fewer than 200 sessions that closes below its swing low is still an Exit, labelled as swing-low only (C-41). A passing holding gets a stop just under the higher of the swing low and the 200 SMA, and is trimmed until the drop from price to stop is at most 1% of capital and no holding exceeds 20% of capital. The legacy book has its own 3% open-risk budget, separate from new trades, and the 10-stock maximum is the target for the whole book. Trims sell the oldest lots first (FIFO), so loss lots book a tax loss first. Legacy trailing (copied from the app Rules tab, OQ-7): kept winners follow the week-8 trail rules from day one. Every weekend, raise each legacy stop to the higher of the 10-week average and the last swing low. Exit on a weekly close below the 10-week average (rule 6) and sell a third on a climax (rule 7). Stops never move down. Tighten first, trim second: if the combined give-back is above 3% of capital, first tighten stops to the 10-week-average level; trim only if the book is still above 3% after that, starting with the largest single give-back. The give-back is the combined drop from today's prices to the stops across all kept holdings; a holding whose stop is above its cost puts no capital at risk. The legacy book does not count toward the open-risk cap for new trades, but it counts toward the 10-stock book. Concentration applies to it too: kept holdings count toward 3 per sector and 2 per industry. For a crowded group keep at most three, the strongest on the exit test, and on a tie sell first those whose tax is already covered.&#32;

**Risk halving:** risk per trade halves automatically after three closed new trades in a row below 0R, or while new-trade equity is 5% or more below its peak (that case ends when equity recovers); the three-losses case ends after two winners close. India VIX above 20 halves it again.

**Checklist rules not stated elsewhere:** every trade needs an RS leader (India: top 20% of Nifty 500 on 3- or 6-month return; US: RS line against SPY rising or at a new high). The stop must sit at a chart level, at least 1.5 × ATR away and within the setup's maximum width. Never add to a loser. The checklist is the app's 60-second check, and one failed item means skip.

**Equity basis for the journal tests:** the fresh-money tests, the 8% drawdown test and the 5% halving trigger are computed on new-trade equity only: starting capital plus the profit and loss of closed new trades, as the app's equity curve does. The legacy book is not in these numbers, so a bad week in the legacy holdings cannot fail the system test; track its give-back separately.

**MAE and MFE** are computed from evening closes (the best and worst close, in R), not from intraday highs and lows. A close-based MAE understates how often a stop was nearly hit, so correct it from the chart when closing a trade.

## Exits, journal and review

**Exit rules** (applied in order every evening to every open position; written for India with a broker GTT, the US equivalent is a stop order):

| # | Trigger | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Buy order filled | Same evening place a stop sell for the full quantity at the stop, no limit price |
| 2 | Price hits the stop | The stop sells. Never cancel it or move it lower |
| 3 | Failed breakout: closes back below the pivot within 3 sessions | Sell next morning |
| 4 | Time stop: 10 sessions without a close at +1R | Sell, the capital is idle |
| 5 | A close at +2R or better | Sell one third, move the stop on the rest to entry |
| 6 | Trail, once rule 5 has sold the third (C-68): weeks 1–7 first daily close below the 20 EMA; from week 8 first weekly close below the 10-week average | Sell the rest next morning |
| 7 | Extended, once rule 5 has sold the third: close 20% or more above the 20 EMA, or the biggest up-day of the move after 3+ weeks of gains | Sell another third |
| 8 | Results due within 2 sessions | Gain below 2R: exit. 2R or more: hold with the stop at entry |
| 9 | Regime drops a level | Positions below +1R: stop to today's low or the 10 EMA, whichever is higher. Above +1R keep the stop. No new buys |

Volume tests used by setups: breakout day at least 1.5× the stock's 50-day average volume; IPO base breakout 2×; earnings gap 3×. Setup 3 test (copied from the app Rules tab, OQ-7): a gap of 5% or more on 3× the 50-day average volume on results day, and the results show 25% or more sales or profit growth. Entry is the next day: trigger = gap-day high + 0.2%, limit = trigger + 1.5%. Stop = the gap-day low; skip the trade if the stop is more than 8% away, or if the gap came from a deal, block trade or rumour rather than results. For index distribution and follow-through days, volume only has to exceed the previous session; no session is skipped in either market (C-73).

**India-only mechanics:** skip stocks in the 2%, 5% or 10% price bands and any stock that hit a lower circuit in the last 3 months. After any bonus, split or ex-date, re-place stops at the adjusted price.

**US differences in exits:** the trail is a weekly close below the 10-week average from the start, so winners can run for months and approach the 24-month long-term tax clock. US sells go in before the next US open, and a stop is a stop order, not a GTT. The circuit-band and ASM rules are India only.

**Journal.** Each trade logs date, stock, setup, sector rank, regime, planned entry, fill, slippage, stop, shares, 1R, exit date and price, exit rule, R-multiple, MAE, MFE, whether rules were followed, and a note. Skipped setups are logged with the reason.

**Monthly review and the fresh-money test.** Fresh money is added only after at least 20 closed trades over at least 3 months pass all four tests:

| Test | Pass mark |
| --- | --- |
| Expectancy per trade | +0.3R or better |
| Average loss | 1.2R or smaller |
| Rule breaks | 2 or fewer in the last 20 trades |
| Drawdown from equity peak | Under 8% |

The tests also run per setup. A setup that fails alone is dropped. When all four pass in total, risk per trade may rise to 1% (India Green only; the US stays at 0.5%) and fresh money goes in over three monthly tranches, with the review re-run after each.
